# CLIENT TRANSLATION

Status: Active v2.0  
Purpose: Define how Stranger Than Paradise / Paradise Lobby logic translates into real client work without forcing every project to become literal Paradise Lobby canon.

---

## 1. Purpose

Paradise Lobby is the strictest laboratory.
Client work is the translated application.

The goal is not to make every client look like a Paradise Lobby government file.
The goal is to translate the strongest parts of the system into usable, sellable, memorable visual outputs.

---

## 2. Translation model

### Internal source
- Stranger Than Paradise = world container
- Paradise Lobby = administrative / residue lab

### External application
Client work becomes:
- release worlds
- motion campaigns
- poster systems
- identity fragments
- print studies
- artifact-led promotional sets
- dossier-based rollout sequences

Rule:
Client work is **informed by** the system, not trapped inside it.

---

## 3. What always carries over

These qualities should remain visible in most client work.

### Always transferable
- atmosphere with control
- system logic
- typographic pressure
- sequence thinking
- tactile proof / friction
- archive/evidence sensibility
- confidence in cropping and withholding
- image-to-world transformation
- print awareness
- motion-to-still continuity

These are the real differentiators.

---

## 4. What does NOT need to carry over literally

Do not force these onto all client work:
- case numbering everywhere
- OCR and barcodes in every project
- administrative residue as literal surface language
- full Paradise Lobby legal / intake behavior
- canon-only wording
- full no-explanation doctrine in every commissioned piece

Those belong to the lab, not automatically to all deliverables.

---

## 5. Translation levels

### Level 1 — Surface translation
Use selected aesthetic features only:
- texture
- type logic
- damage/folds
- restraint
- evidence-like image handling
- print/scan materiality

Best for:
- fashion
- editorial
- product campaigns

### Level 2 — Structural translation
Use system logic in layout and rollout:
- paired assets
- document-like sequencing
- classification language
- progressive reveal
- release as dossier
- case-study carousel structure

Best for:
- music releases
- exhibitions
- identity systems

### Level 3 — Deep translation
Use near-canonical pressure where appropriate:
- withheld information
- filing logic
- artifact drops
- directive language
- administrative pressure
- stronger evidence behavior

Best for:
- art projects
- cultural institutions
- experimental campaigns
- self-initiated work

---

## 6. Best-fit client categories

### Strongest fit
- musicians and labels
- underground or culture-led fashion brands
- exhibitions / curators / screenings
- editorial / publishing projects
- art direction-heavy launches

### Medium fit
- product-based brands seeking a harder image language
- campaign launches needing a stronger world layer
- cultural start-ups with concept-heavy identity

### Weak fit
- generic corporate communication
- soft wellness brands
- purely friendly lifestyle branding
- clients who need clean, generic platform-native work only

Rule:
Do not bend the system to weak-fit clients just to say yes.

## 7. Flagship translated offer

### RELEASE WORLD SYSTEM
This is the primary client-facing offer.

Possible deliverables:
- 1 visual direction
- 2-4 posters
- 1 motion poster / reel
- 4-8 rollout assets
- optional dossier/document application
- optional artifact/detail support
- optional print-study or edition proof

Why this should lead:
- easy to understand
- uses your full stack
- naturally generates portfolio content
- can be sold as both spec and client work
- bridges AI source generation with real design discipline

## 8. Supporting offers

For clients who need movement first.

### Print Study / Artifact Add-On
For clients who want the release to feel physical, handled, or collectible.

### Edition Development Add-On
Translate a campaign into a small print/object release.

### Dossier Rollout Add-On
Adds case-study style carousel sequencing, detail crops, routing logic, or release documentation behavior.

## 9. Client-facing language

Use language clients understand.

### Good client-facing terms
- visual world
- release system
- campaign rollout
- motion poster
- poster series
- print study
- visual identity layer
- art-directed rollout
- tactile campaign assets
- release-ready world

### Terms to use carefully
- archive
- evidence
- dossier
- directive
- artifact

### Terms to avoid as lead language
- Paradise Lobby
- System C
- residue
- incomprehension
- canon doctrine
- administrative processing language as internal jargon

Reason:
Clients buy outcomes, not your internal mythology.

---

## 10. How to pitch the work

Do not say:
- I make weird experimental government-file aesthetics
- I build fictional bureaucratic worlds
- I do AI art plus graphic design plus motion plus whatever you need

Say:
- I build release-ready visual worlds across posters, motion, print studies, and campaign assets
- I can turn a concept or source image into a complete rollout language
- My work combines AI image generation with strong graphic finishing, sequence logic, and physical proof

## 12. Case studies are sales tools

A case study is not extra content.
It is one of the strongest selling forms.

A translated case study may include:
- source scene(s)
- AI image generation seed
- modified stills
- typography development
- motion frames
- print studies
- tactile proof
- final rollout assets

Rule:
If the case study proves the machine and looks hireable, publish it.

---

## 13. Internal test before adapting a client brief

Ask:
1. Which part of the STP/PL system strengthens this client?
2. Which part would overcomplicate or obscure the work?
3. Does the output still look hireable, not just interesting?
4. Can the client understand the value without understanding the full lore?
5. Can this project generate a usable public case study afterward?

If 4 is no, translation is failing.
If 5 is no, the project may still be valid — but it is a weaker strategic fit.

---

## 14. Anti-drift rules

- Do not force Paradise Lobby skin onto every project.
- Do not erase your differentiators to satisfy generic briefs.
- Do not speak to clients in internal world jargon.
- Do not reduce the system to trend aesthetics.
- Do not treat client work as fully separate from your authorship.
- Do not accept work that destroys your public positioning just to stay busy.

---

## 15. Core translation statement

**The client does not need the whole world. They need the strongest translated pressure of the world, shaped into a release-ready outcome.**

That is the correct balance.
